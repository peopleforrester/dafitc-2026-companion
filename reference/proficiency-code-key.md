# Air Force Proficiency Code Key

Reproduced verbatim from published Career Field Education and Training Plans.
Confirmed 26 August 2026 against **CFETP 2R1X1** (Maintenance Production
Management) and **CFETP 3S2X1** (Education and Training). The wording is
identical in both. [S22]

---

## What a CFETP is

The Career Field Education and Training Plan is the Air Force's training master
document. One per Air Force Specialty Code, published by HQ USAF, signed by order
of the Secretary of the Air Force.

Each one contains a Specialty Training Standard: the full list of tasks an Airman
in that career field must be able to do. Every task carries a proficiency code
stating the level required at the 3-, 5- and 7-skill levels.

Placed in the AF Form 623 training record, the CFETP becomes that Airman's Job
Qualification Standard. A trainer and a certifier initial each task.

It is not a curriculum document. It is an assessment instrument with a named
observer and a signature.

---

## Task performance levels

**1. Extremely Limited**
Can do simple parts of the task. Needs to be told or shown how to do most of the
task.

**2. Partially Proficient**
Can do most parts of the task. Needs only help on hardest parts.

**3. Competent**
Can do all parts of the task. Needs only a spot check of completed work.

**4. Highly Proficient**
Can do the complete task quickly and accurately. Can tell or show others how to
do the task.

---

## Task knowledge levels

**a. Nomenclature**
Can name parts, tools, and simple facts about the task.

**b. Procedures**
Can determine step by step procedures for doing the task.

**c. Operating Principles**
Can identify why and when the task must be done and why each step is needed.

**d. Advanced Theory**
Can predict, isolate, and resolve problems about the task.

---

## Subject knowledge levels

**A. Facts**
Can identify basic facts and terms about the subject.

**B. Principles**
Can identify relationship of basic facts and state general principles about the
subject.

**C. Analysis**
Can analyze facts and principles and draw conclusions about the subject.

**D. Evaluation**
Can evaluate conditions and make proper decisions about the subject.

---

## How the codes combine

A task knowledge value may be used alone or with a task performance value.
**1b** is a real placement: knows the procedures, can do the simple parts.

In plain language, the two scales ask four questions.

Do they know the words. Do they know the steps. Do they understand the principles
well enough to adapt. Do they understand the theory well enough to troubleshoot.

**Expertise is the knowledge. Experience is its application.**

---

## The harder standard underneath

For on-the-job training, tasks are qualified to the **go/no-go** level. Quoted
verbatim from CFETP 2R1X1:

> "Go" means the individual can perform the task **without assistance** and meets
> local requirements for **accuracy, timeliness, and correct procedures.**

That sentence is a better working definition of demonstrated competency than
anything a vendor will hand you, and it is already yours.

## Independent corroboration

Two bodies of assessment research arrive at the same shape, which is worth
knowing because it means this is not a quirk of military paperwork.

**Miller's pyramid** (1990) separates *Knows*, *Knows how*, *Shows how*, and
*Does*. The lower two assess knowledge, the upper two assess behaviour. *Shows
how* is performance in a controlled environment; *Does* is performance
unsupervised in real practice. [S25]

**Entrustable Professional Activities** operationalise it. The key distinction:
competencies are qualities of individuals, but EPAs are **units of work**. You do
not assess competency in the abstract. You name a unit of work and record how
much supervision it still needs, on an entrustment-supervision scale. [S26]

Both are the CFETP task performance scale, arrived at independently.
