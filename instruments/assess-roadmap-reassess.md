# Assess, roadmap, reassess

How to turn competency into a measured delta instead of a claim.

---

## Why a claim is not enough

Competence with AI is the one capability people cannot self-assess.

In a randomized controlled trial, 16 experienced open-source developers worked
246 tasks on mature projects they had averaged five years in. Before starting,
they forecast AI would cut completion time by 24 percent. Afterwards, they
estimated it had cut it by 20 percent.

It increased completion time by **19 percent**. [S7]

The experts were wrong in the same direction. Economists predicted 39 percent
faster. Machine learning researchers predicted 38 percent.

METR redesigned the experiment in February 2026 rather than repeat it. Too many
developers declined to take part because they did not want to work without AI,
which biases the result; the pay rate had dropped from $150 to $50 an hour; and
their own time-on-task measurements proved unreliable.

That last point is the argument, not a footnote. Inside twelve months the tools
were absorbed so completely that the counterfactual became hard to buy.

---

## The three stages

**Knowledge training.** Generalized knowledge. Necessary, not sufficient.
Proven by completion.

**General skill acquisition.** Skill that transfers, and is worth having on its
own. Proven by assessment.

**Context-specific outcomes.** Demonstrated competency in a specific situation.
Proven by a capstone in your own environment.

Knowledge is not skill, and skill is not an outcome.

---

## The method

**Assess.** Baseline the person against a named task, using the published
standard for that task. Not a survey. An evaluator, against a criterion.

**Roadmap.** A role path plus coaching at the point of need, grounded in your own
doctrine and TTPs, delivered during the task rather than three weeks earlier in
a classroom.

**Reassess.** The same task, the same standard, the same instrument. Report the
change.

Assessments are light. Before the task and after it, about fifteen minutes each.

---

## What the delta looks like

Using the [proficiency code key](../reference/proficiency-code-key.md):

**Before the path**
Task: named, from their own work.
Knowledge: **b**, procedures. Can follow the steps.
Task performance: **2**, partially proficient. Needs help on the hardest parts.

**After the capstone**
Task: the same task.
Knowledge: **c**, operating principles. Knows why each step is needed.
Task performance: **3**, competent. Needs only a spot check.

The delta is **2b to 3c** on a named task, assessed twice by an evaluator against
a published standard.

Same instrument both times. **The delta is the deliverable, not the
certificate.**

## This is already departmental policy

Demonstrated competency rather than a certificate is not a vendor's opinion.
DoDM 8140.03, the Cyberspace Workforce Qualification and Management Program,
is a mandatory competency-based qualification model: foundational qualification
within nine months, demonstrated on-the-job readiness within twelve. The DoD
Cyber Workforce Framework it runs on already contains data and AI work roles.

If you are asked why demonstrated competency needs proving rather than
asserting, that is the precedent. See [official
documents](../reference/official-documents.md).

## The capstone

One person. Their workflow. Their tools. A measured result.

- **Their own workflow**, not a sandbox exercise.
- **Their own tools and their own corpus.** Doctrine and TTPs for operators,
  defensive playbooks and response procedures for cyber defenders.
- **Measured against the workflow's existing measures**, which means the training
  provider does not get to define success.
- **Evaluated by someone who can tell them whether they actually did it right.**

In many cases the capstone is a git repository.

It is usually simulated rather than run in production, and deliberately so. In
many cases you cannot get access to the production environment, and more to the
point you do not want to. Risk goes up the moment training touches production.

**The vendor test:** do not deal with a vendor who cannot give you a simulated,
or real, in-your-environment production experience. A vendor who does not support
that outcome does not understand that seat time will not help you.
