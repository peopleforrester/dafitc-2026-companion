# Capstone specification

A capstone is the artefact that proves a context-specific outcome. This is what
one looks like written down, so you can hand it to a training provider as a
requirement instead of describing it in a meeting.

Fill it in before you buy anything.

---

## 1. The person

Name or role: _______________________________________

Their AFSC or duty position: ________________________

Current placement on the task below, from the [proficiency code
key](../reference/proficiency-code-key.md): ____________

Who assessed that, and when: ________________________

## 2. The task

Name a single task from their real work. Not a topic, not a skill area. A task
someone could watch them do.

Task: _______________________________________________

Where it appears in their CFETP or duty position requirements: ______________

Why this task: _______________________________________

## 3. The environment

The capstone runs in the learner's own environment, with their own tools, or in
a simulation of it faithful enough that the skills transfer.

Tools and systems they will use: _____________________

Corpus or reference material: ________________________
(Doctrine and TTPs for operators. Defensive playbooks and response procedures
for cyber defenders.)

Simulated or production: _____________________________

If production, say why that risk is acceptable. Usually it is not. Training that
touches production raises risk the moment it starts, and a capstone is not worth
a live system.

## 4. The measure

This is the part vendors skip. The measure comes from the workflow, not from the
training.

What the workflow already measures: __________________

Baseline value, measured not estimated: ______________

How that baseline was captured: ______________________

Target after the capstone: ___________________________

**The training provider does not get to define this.** If they do, they are
grading their own work.

## 5. The evaluator

Who confirms the work was actually done right: ________

Their qualification to judge it: ______________________

An evaluator must be able to tell the learner whether they did it right, not
just whether they finished. That normally means someone already at proficiency 3
or 4 on the same task.

## 6. The artefact

What the learner hands over at the end.

Form: _______________________________________________
(Often a git repository. Sometimes a changed procedure, a working automation, a
completed analysis, or a documented run.)

Where it will live afterwards: _______________________

## 7. The reassessment

Same task. Same standard. Same instrument. Same evaluator if possible.

Placement after: _____________________________________

Delta: _______________________________________________

Date: ________________________________________________

---

## What "done" looks like

A capstone is complete when all of these are true.

- The learner performed a named task from their own work.
- They used their own tools against their own corpus.
- An evaluator confirmed it against a published standard.
- The workflow's own measure moved, and you can say by how much.
- The delta is recorded against the same instrument used at baseline.

If the last two are missing you have an exercise, not an outcome. That is worth
having, but do not call it demonstrated competency.

---

## Using this with a vendor

Hand them sections 2 through 6 filled in and ask whether they can support it. A
provider who cannot give you a simulated or real in-your-environment experience
cannot deliver a capstone, whatever they call their final module.

The rest of the questions are in [questions for a
vendor](questions-for-a-vendor.md).
